10 Reasons to Visit Igalo & Herceg Novi

Updated: July 2026 7 min read Igalo & Herceg Novi, Montenegro

Looking for the right place for your next holiday? Igalo and Herceg Novi in Montenegro offer a combination that is genuinely hard to find elsewhere on the Mediterranean: therapeutic mud beaches, 2,700 years of history at the mouth of the Bay of Kotor, clear Adriatic water, excellent food - and prices that still make sense. Here are ten reasons Herceg Novi belongs near the top of your list.

1

Beaches for every taste

The Municipality of Herceg Novi has 62 registered beaches along 25 km of coastline - varied enough to suit almost anyone. Want sand and shallow water that works for small children? Head for Blatna Plaža in Igalo. Prefer an organised beach with water sports? Raffaello in Topla is the local favourite. After hidden coves with olive shade? Take a boat to Žanjice on the Luštica peninsula.

The Adriatic here reaches 22-26 °C in summer, and the swimming season runs up to five months, from May through September.

2

A proper Mediterranean climate

Igalo has a classic Mediterranean climate - hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters. July and August are the warmest months, averaging 25-30 °C with around 10.7 hours of sunshine a day. Nights rarely drop below 20 °C, which is what makes long dinners by the water so easy here.

If you would rather avoid the crowds, the shoulder seasons (May-June and September-October) deliver 15-22 °C, still-warm sea and a considerably calmer town. Winter stays mild at 10-12 °C - which is exactly why mimosa flowers here in mid-February, when the whole town celebrates the Mimosa Festival, Montenegro's oldest festival.

3

2,700 years of history

Herceg Novi itself is one of the younger fortified towns on the Adriatic - founded in 1382 by the Bosnian king Tvrtko I - but it sits on a crossroads of empires that shaped the whole Mediterranean. Romans, Byzantines, Bosnians, Ottomans, Venetians, Austrians and the French all ruled here at different points, and each left something behind in the streets, churches, mosques and fortresses.

The Old Town is compact and pleasant to explore on foot. The landmarks worth your time are the Kanli Kula fortress (which hosts open-air theatre under the stars in summer), the clock tower on Belavista square, and Forte Mare, the fortress that rises straight out of the sea. The Orthodox Savina Monastery, one of Montenegro's most important spiritual sites, is a 20-minute walk along the promenade.

4

Healing mud, health and wellness

This is what makes Igalo genuinely unusual on the Adriatic. The therapeutic radioactive mud of Blatna Plaža - confirmed by the famous Vichy laboratory in France as far back as 1930 - is one of very few such natural deposits in Europe. It is mineral-rich and mildly radioactive in the therapeutic sense, and it is used for rheumatism, arthritis and musculoskeletal conditions.

Mud therapy on Blatna Plaža is completely free. You apply the mud, let it dry in the sun, and rinse off in the sea. The nearby Institut Dr Simo Milošević - directly opposite our apartments - offers certified medical programmes that combine the same mud with physiotherapy and hydrotherapy.

Staying at Monako Apartments? The Institut Dr Simo Milošević is right across the road and Blatna Plaža is 100 m from the door - free mud therapy within reach every day of your stay. Coming for a longer course of treatment or to overwinter? There is also monthly apartment rental in Igalo from €500 all-inclusive, available September to June.

5

Fresh seafood and Montenegrin cooking

Montenegrin cooking draws from both the Adriatic coast and the Balkan hinterland, which produces a table that is simple and deeply satisfying at once. Along the promenade from Igalo to Herceg Novi you will find fish landed that morning - sea bass, sea bream, squid and octopus salad - alongside njeguški pršut, local cheeses and grilled meat from inland.

Prices sit well below comparable Adriatic resorts in Croatia or Italy, and the konoba tradition means small family-run places still outnumber the tourist restaurants.

6

Outdoor activities on land and water

If you want more than a sunbed, Herceg Novi delivers. Kayaking and diving in the bay, boat trips to the Blue Cave and Mamula, hiking on the slopes of Orjen behind the town, and cycling along the Pet Danica promenade - a flat 7 km seafront path that links Igalo to the Old Town.

7

Nightlife that stays in proportion

The densest concentration of bars and clubs sits near the marina and the town centre, where the atmosphere stays lively through the season. You will find everything from pop and rock to house and local folk, plus quieter bars strung along the beachfront if you want something calmer.

Worth knowing: nightlife in Herceg Novi is mostly a May to September affair. Outside those months the town is much quieter, though a handful of bars stay open year-round.

8

Prices that still make sense

Holidays on the Adriatic have grown expensive, but Herceg Novi remains one of the better-value stretches of coast. Accommodation spans every budget, and food and drink stay noticeably cheaper than in Croatia or Italy. Off-season rates drop 30-40% below summer, which is part of why the town has become popular with long-stay visitors and remote workers.

Booking direct with an owner rather than through a platform typically avoids the 15-20% commission built into the listed price.

9

Landscape you actually notice

Herceg Novi sits at the mouth of the Bay of Kotor, with the Orjen massif rising steeply behind it. The result is a town built in terraces down to the water, with clear sea in front and mountains at your back. The town is known as the "city of a thousand steps" and as a botanical garden of the Adriatic - subtropical planting brought back by sailors thrives in the mild climate.

10

A base for the rest of Montenegro

Igalo is a practical base for exploring. Kotor is about 40 minutes away, Perast and Our Lady of the Rocks a little further, Budva around an hour. The Croatian border is roughly 10 minutes from Igalo, putting Dubrovnik within an easy day trip. Tivat airport is about 30 minutes; Dubrovnik airport around 45 minutes, border crossing permitting.

And if the holiday turns into a longer stay, there is monthly apartment rental in Igalo at €500 all-inclusive, available September to June.

Igalo and Herceg Novi are not the loudest destination on the Adriatic, and that is largely the point. Between the beaches, the healing mud, the fortresses, the food and the mountains behind the bay, there is more here than a week comfortably holds - at prices that have not yet caught up with the rest of the coast.
