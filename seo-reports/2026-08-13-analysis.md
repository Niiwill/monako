**Both intents have plateaued.** Summer peaked at 6,269 in the week of
Jul 13–19 and has oscillated 5,876 / 6,228 / 5,993 since — flat for four
weeks, not climbing. Commercial peaked a week earlier and is down 13% from
its high.

### Why my Aug 12 report got this wrong

I read demand from a monthly table, which showed summer at 731 impressions/day
in July and 822/day in August and concluded it was still climbing. But July's
average is dragged down by the first two weeks of the month, when the season
was still ramping (3,489 then 5,626 impressions). Comparing a ramping month
against a plateaued one manufactures growth that is not there.

Monthly aggregates hide turning points. Weekly does not. The corrected
monthly view still reads Aug > Jul; only the weekly series shows the peak.

### What this does and does not change

The April–June booking-window conclusion **still holds** — it rests on the
commercial curve, which peaked Jul 6–12 and is now falling, exactly as
described. What changes is the informational side: the autumn decline in
beach traffic starts from now, not from September. Plan for the off-season
content to carry weight sooner.

## 8. Method note

Second correction in two days caused by reading an aggregate instead of a
series — first the incomplete final day, now a monthly average spanning a
ramp. Both produced a confident statement in the wrong direction.

Adding to the standing run alongside the CTR-anomaly sweep:

> **Never call a turning point from a monthly or 28-day aggregate.** A period
> that contains a ramp will always understate its own end state. Read the
> weekly series before claiming a peak, a trough, or a trend.
