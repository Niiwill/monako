### Read

1. **The profile is a phone-booking channel, not a website funnel.**
   156 calls vs 94 website clicks — **1.66 calls per website click**, about
   5.2 calls/day in peak season. These are the highest-intent contacts the
   business gets anywhere; nothing on the website matches that intent
   density.
2. **Messages = 0 and Bookings = 0.** Two conversion channels producing
   nothing — almost certainly not enabled on the profile. Messaging in
   particular suits foreign and younger guests who will not place an
   international call. This is the single biggest zero-cost gap found today.
3. **94% mobile**, mirroring GSC's ~90% mobile split. Consistent audience.
4. **39% of views come from Maps**, a surface the website cannot influence
   at all — only the profile (categories, photos, reviews, posts) can.
5. **2.96% action rate** is respectable for lodging; the constraint is view
   volume and channel coverage, not conversion of what arrives.

### Scale in context

- Organic site: 94,089 impressions → 6,032 clicks (27d)
- GBP: 12,124 views → 359 direct actions (30d)
- GBP website clicks (94) are only **14%** of homepage organic clicks (690),
  so GBP is not a meaningful traffic source — it is a **conversion** source.

### Recommended GBP actions (off-repo, owner)

1. **Enable messaging** — highest-value, zero-cost. Set expectations with a
   fast auto-reply.
2. **Add a booking/reservation link** so the Bookings channel stops reading
   zero; point it at the homepage `#cjenovnik` section.
3. **Keep review velocity up** — reviews are the legitimate route to visible
   star ratings, since own-site rating markup cannot produce them (2019
   self-serving review policy).
4. Photos and Posts weekly during season — both feed Maps ranking, which is
   39% of profile views.

## Addendum — reconciliation with the NAP/entity audit

The owner confirmed PR #65's title changes were deliberate: syncing website
data with the GBP NAP. That is correct and worth protecting — 39% of profile
views come from Maps, where entity consistency drives ranking.

PR #66 was revised to respect the audit rather than compete with it:

| Version | Title | Chars |
|---|---|---|
| PR #65 (audit) | `Igalo, Crna Gora – 10 Razloga za Odmor \| Apartmani Igalo Monako` | 63 |
| PR #66 first pass | `Igalo, Crna Gora – Plaže, Blato i 10 Razloga \| Monako` | 53 |
| **PR #66 final** | `Igalo – Plaže, Blato i 10 Razloga \| Apartmani Igalo Monako` | **58** |

The first pass used a short `| Monako` token, which broke the convention the
audit established across all 19 titles (`<topic> | Apartmani Igalo Monako`).
The final version keeps the full brand suffix and moves the query keywords to
the front, where they remain visible before truncation. No NAP field, schema
node, or contact detail is touched — the diff is one line.

### Two things the audit surfaced that belong in this tracking record

1. **C2 — self-serving `aggregateRating`/`review` removed from all business
   nodes.** This independently confirms the diagnosis recorded on 2026-07-18:
   own-site rating markup cannot produce SERP stars (2019 policy). The
   experiment closed on 2026-07-19 for that reason; the markup is now gone,
   which is the correct end state. Visible copy was also reconciled to the
   real 71 Google reviews.
2. **Schema type has flip-flopped:** `LodgingBusiness` → `VacationRental`
   (PR #52, Jul 16) → `LodgingBusiness` (PR #65, Jul 25). Both are defensible;
   stability now matters more than the choice. **Recommendation: leave it at
   `LodgingBusiness` and stop changing it** — repeated type changes reset
   Google's entity understanding.
