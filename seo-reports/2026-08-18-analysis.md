which kinds of pages Google sends traffic to.

## 2. ⭐ Verdict: the meta hypothesis is rejected

| | Clicks | Impressions | CTR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aug 4–10 (pre, 7d) | 60 | 1,720 | 3.49% |
| **Aug 11–15 (post, 5d)** | **33** | **1,163** | **2.84%** |
| 95% CI | | | **[1.88%, 3.79%]** |

| Threshold | Value | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmation | ≥ 4.50% | **outside the CI** |
| Rejection | ≤ 3.60% | point estimate below it |
| Pre-regression baseline | 5.79% | far outside |

Daily since recrawl: 2.92%, 2.80%, 3.23%, 2.63%, **2.49%** — drifting down,
not recovering.

**Conclusion, stated plainly:** on 2026-08-11 I wrote that shortening the meta
descriptions on five pages cost ~82 clicks/week, and I recommended reverting
them. The revert is now in Google's index on the page carrying half that
estimate, and CTR did not move. **The diagnosis was wrong.** The reverts were
harmless — they restored text with measured CTR — but they fixed nothing,
because the meta was never the cause.

### What the cause actually was

Yesterday's YoY analysis supplies it. Across 37 queries where we hold
position <2 in both years, impressions are flat (−2.5%) and CTR fell from
36.26% to 21.22%. Those queries sit on `plaze-herceg-novi` — **the page I
used as the control group, whose meta was never touched.** The control was
not a control; it was subject to the same force.

That force is SERP composition: features above the organic block taking
clicks that used to reach position 1. It hit the whole informational estate
at once in the same period, which is exactly what a July CTR decline across
five similar pages looks like when you have no valid comparison group.

**Two pages recrawled yesterday** (`restorani-igalo`, `restaurants-herceg-novi`,
both Aug 17), so their reverts are now indexed too. Data ends Aug 15, so
there is nothing to read yet. I am not expecting anything, and I will report
what arrives either way.

queries — "which beaches", "what to see", "where to eat" — are exactly what
gets answered above the results. Commercial and event queries are not.

### What this changes about the plan

- **Stop measuring success on the informational estate.** Those pages are
  losing to a structural change we cannot out-optimise, at positions that
  are already excellent.
- **The growth is where we already pointed the work** — New Year, mimosa,
  banja, the homepage, the GBP surface. That allocation was right, and the
  YoY numbers are the first proof of it.
- **`atrakcije-herceg-novi` at −80.3% is the worst page on the site YoY.**
  That is the `herceg novi` head-term loss from February compounding with
  the informational decline. It needs a decision about whether it is worth
  further investment at all.

## 6. Method note

The pre-registered test did its job: it produced a clean rejection of my own
recommendation on a fixed date, with no room to argue. Everything I got wrong
in the last fortnight came from asserting a mechanism without a control; the
one thing that held up is the one where the thresholds were written before
the data existed.

The YoY rule added yesterday has already paid for itself — the page-level
split in section 3 is the most actionable thing in this report, and it was
invisible in five weeks of month-over-month comparisons.
