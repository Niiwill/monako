**Statistically, at n = 962 impressions:**

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Observed CTR | 2.91% |
| 95% CI | **[1.85%, 3.97%]** |
| Confirmation threshold 4.50% | **outside the interval** |

The pre-registered read is still Aug 18, and the rejection threshold is
formally still inside the interval, so I am not declaring the test closed.
But the **confirmation** side can already be ruled out: there is no plausible
reading of this data in which the restored meta brought CTR back to 4.5%,
let alone 5.79%.

### What this means

The diagnosis that drove five page reverts — that shortening those meta
descriptions cost ~82 clicks/week — is very likely wrong. The reverts were
harmless (they restored states with measured CTR) but they did not fix
anything, because the meta was not the cause.

The leading alternative is the one visible in every other section of these
reports: **a seasonal SERP shift**. The same five pages are informational
guides whose CTR fell as summer demand peaked and the SERPs above them filled
with competing seasonal content. That would produce exactly what we saw —
CTR falling at flat or improving position across a group of similar pages at
the same time — without any of our edits being involved.

Second piece of evidence, unchanged from yesterday: `restorani-igalo`
improved to 3.11% CTR this week, **above its 2.78% baseline**, and it has not
been recrawled since **Jul 11**. Its shortened meta is still the one in
Google's index. A page cannot recover from a cause that is still present.

I will write the formal verdict tomorrow against the pre-registered
thresholds.

## 7. Method note

Three calls corrected in one report: the meta hypothesis, the image sitemap
attribution, and the crawl-priority explanation for the silent articles.

The common thread is that all three were **plausible mechanisms asserted
without a control**. The meta story had a before/after but no comparison
group that kept its meta and shared the seasonal exposure. The image
attribution never checked whether the gain predated the change. The
crawl-priority story never tested the pages that had no links but recovered
anyway.

The one that is holding up is the one where I wrote the thresholds down
first.

> **Before attributing an improvement or a decline to a change, ask what else
> was happening to comparable pages in the same period.** A mechanism that
> sounds right and a before/after that points the right way are not evidence
> until something that did *not* get the change is measured alongside it.
