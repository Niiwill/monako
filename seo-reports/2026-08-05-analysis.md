## 1. ⚠ Correction: yesterday's headline finding was measured wrong

Yesterday's strategic review concluded that commercial traffic was flat
(−2%) while informational grew 161%, and framed that as the central problem.
Today's run initially made it look worse:

| Metric | Prev 28d | This 28d |
|---|---|---|
| Non-brand commercial clicks (organic) | 244 | 196 (**−20%**) |
| Non-brand commercial CTR | 2.51% | 2.08% |

**Before reporting a 20% decline in new-customer acquisition, I decomposed
it — and the decline is not real.**

### The decomposition

| Component | Change |
|---|---|
| Impressions | −3.0% |
| Average position | **5.46 → 5.30 (improved)** |
| Organic CTR | **−17.2%** |

Position improved and impressions held, yet CTR collapsed. Per-query, the
pattern is unmistakable:

| Query | Clicks | Position |
|---|---|---|
| blatna plaza igalo smestaj | 27 → 9 | 1.9 → **1.9 (identical)** |
| apartmani igalo | 43 → 26 | 5.5 → **4.5 (better)** |
| apartmani igalo stara banja | 6 → 3 | 6.2 → **2.4 (much better)** |
| blatna plaza igalo smestaj cene | 40 → 32 | 2.6 → **2.3 (better)** |

**Clicks falling while position improves is not a ranking problem.** It is
the signature of something new appearing above the organic listing.

### What appeared: the local pack

The GBP landing URL began serving these exact queries on **Jul 25** — the
same date the CTR broke. Splitting the same metric across both surfaces:

| Period | Organic/day | GBP-URL/day | **Total/day** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jul 11–24 (before) | 8.8 | 0 | **8.8** |
| Jul 25 – Aug 4 (after) | 7.2 | 2.9 | **10.1** |

**Non-brand commercial acquisition is up 15%, not down 20.** The business now
appears twice on these SERPs; the local pack takes a share of the clicks, and
GSC files them under a different URL. Measuring organic alone counted the
loss and missed the gain.

And this understates it further: **GBP profile-button clicks are reported in
GBP, not GSC**, so 2.9/day is a floor, not the true figure.

### Metric definition, corrected

From today, the headline metric is **non-brand commercial clicks across both
surfaces — organic homepage + GBP URL.** Yesterday's definition (organic only)
would have had us "fixing" a decline that was actually growth, and would have
made every future local-pack gain look like a loss.

**Baseline: 10.1/day.**

## 2. What this changes about strategy

Yesterday I argued the local pack was the achievable commercial channel. That
was right, but I did not follow it through to the measurement — and the data
now shows the substitution actively happening:

- Organic commercial CTR is being **cannibalised by the business's own local
  pack**, which is the desired outcome, not a problem.
- The organic homepage's commercial CTR (1.78% on 7,794 impressions) will
  likely keep declining as the pack strengthens. **That number should be
  expected to fall and should not be optimised against.**
- Effort spent lifting the homepage's organic commercial CTR is now partly
  spent competing with the business's own better-placed listing.

**Practical consequence:** the highest-value work remains GBP — reviews,
photos, posts, the still-zero bookings link — because that is the surface
absorbing the demand.

## 6. Method note

Two analyses in two days have now been corrected — the funnel finding
(an `<aside>` filter that hid the existing CTAs) and today's commercial
metric (organic-only measurement that hid the local pack). Both were caught
by decomposing a headline number before acting on it. Both would have led to
wasted work. Decomposition before recommendation is now standing practice in
these reports.
